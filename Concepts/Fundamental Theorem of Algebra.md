# Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

> [!abstract] In one paragraph
> Every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root. Repeatedly factoring out roots, a degree-$n$ polynomial splits into $n$ linear factors, so it has exactly $n$ roots counted with multiplicity.

## Statement

If $p(z)=c_nz^n+\dots+c_1z+c_0$ with $c_i\in\mathbb C$, $n\ge1$, $c_n\ne0$, then there are $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb C$ with
$$p(z)=c_n(z-\alpha_1)\cdots(z-\alpha_n).$$
