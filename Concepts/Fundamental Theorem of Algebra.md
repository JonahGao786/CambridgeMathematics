# Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

Every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root. More fully, if
$$p(z)=c_nz^n+\cdots+c_0,\qquad n\ge1,\quad c_n\ne0,$$
then there are $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb C$ such that
$$p(z)=c_n(z-\alpha_1)\cdots(z-\alpha_n).$$
Thus it has exactly $n$ roots counted with multiplicity.

Sources: [[Vectors and Matrices - Chapter 1 Complex Numbers]], page 5, and the root-existence example in [[Numbers and Sets - Chapter 0 Proofs]], page 3. These sources state the theorem without proving it.
