# Isometries and Dihedral Groups

> [!abstract] In one paragraph
> Distance-preserving bijections of $X\subseteq\mathbb C$ form a group $\operatorname{Isom}(X)$. For the vertices of a regular $n$-gon this is the dihedral group $D_{2n}$: exactly $n$ rotations and $n$ reflections, generated by $r$ and $s$ with $sr=r^{-1}s$.

## Definitions

- **Isometry** of $X$: bijection $f$ with $|f(x)-f(y)|=|x-y|$. → [[Groups - Chapter 1 Examples and Definitions#Definition 1.19 — Isometry|lecture]]
- $X_n=\{e^{2\pi ik/n}\}$ ($n\ge3$); $D_{2n}=\operatorname{Isom}(X_n)$.
- $r_k(z)=e^{2\pi ik/n}z$ (rotations), $s_\ell(z)=e^{2\pi i\ell/n}\bar z$ (reflection in the line through $e^{\pi i\ell/n}$).

## Key results

- **Proposition 1.20.** $\operatorname{Isom}(X)\le\operatorname{Sym}(X)$.
- **Lemma 1.23 — kite.** Equal distances from $z_1$ and from $z_2$ to $w_1,w_2$ $\Rightarrow$ $(z_1-z_2)\perp(w_1-w_2)$.
- **Lemma 1.24 — three points.** An isometry fixing three non-collinear points is the identity. → [[Groups - Chapter 1 Examples and Definitions#Lemma 1.24 — Three point lemma|proof]]
- **Proposition 1.25.** $D_{2n}=\{r_k,s_\ell\}$, $|D_{2n}|=2n$. *Idea:* $f(1)=\zeta^m$, neighbours go to neighbours, so $f$ agrees with $r_m$ or $s_m$ on three points; apply the three point lemma. → [[Groups - Chapter 1 Examples and Definitions#Proposition 1.25 — Elements of the dihedral group|proof]]
- **Lemma 1.26.** $r=r_1$, $s=s_0$ generate, $r_k=r^k$, $s_\ell=r^\ell s$, and $sr=r^{-1}s$; hence $sr^k=r^{-k}s$.
- **Lemma 1.40.** If $a^n=b^2=e$ and $ab=ba^{-1}$ in $G$, then $r^k\mapsto a^k$, $r^ks\mapsto a^kb$ is a homomorphism $D_{2n}\to G$.

## Examples

- $D_6$ = symmetries of the equilateral triangle; $D_8$ (square): 4 rotations, 4 reflections.

## Pitfalls

- $D_{2n}$ has $2n$ elements (notation by order; some texts write $D_n$).
